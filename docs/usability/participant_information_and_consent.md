## Part A — Participant Information Statement

### Project title

Usability evaluation of a Human-AI teaming graphical user interface (GUI) for
reviewing ambulatory and outpatient electroencephalography (EEG).

### What is this document about?

You are invited to take part in a research study that evaluates the **usability** of a
software tool. This Participant Information Statement explains what the study involves so
that you can decide whether you would like to take part. Please read it carefully and ask
the research team any questions you may have before deciding. Taking part is entirely
voluntary. If you decide to take part, you will be asked to sign the Consent Form in
Part B, and you will be given a copy to keep.

### What is the purpose of the study?

The study assesses how easy and intuitive a prototype reviewer interface is to use. The
interface is a desktop application that displays EEG signal traces together with an
overlaid AI-generated seizure-probability strip and a list of proposed candidate events,
and lets a reviewer step through, accept, reject, or adjust those proposed events. The
aim is to gather feedback on the design of the interface — for example, clarity of the
layout, the controls, and the review workflow — so that it can be improved. **The study
evaluates the software, not you.** There are no right or wrong answers and your individual
performance is not being graded or assessed.

### Who is being asked to take part?

We are inviting a small number (approximately 3-5) of non-clinical peer participants —
for example, fellow engineering students or research colleagues — to take part. **You do
not need any clinical, neurology, or EEG-reading expertise to participate.** The study is
about the usability of the software interface, not about making any medical or diagnostic
judgement.

### What does participation involve?

If you agree to take part, you will be asked to attend a single session of approximately
**30 to 45 minutes**, in person or via screen share. During the session you will:

1. Receive a short verbal and/or written orientation to the interface.
2. Work through a set of guided tasks using the software — for example, opening a sample
   recording, adjusting display controls, stepping through proposed events, and
   accepting, rejecting, or editing them.
3. Complete a short questionnaire about your experience (for example, a standard usability
   questionnaire and a few open-ended questions). You may also be asked to "think aloud"
   while you work so that we can understand your reasoning.

With your separate consent (see the checklist in Part B), the session may be
**screen-recorded** and the **time taken** on tasks may be logged, so that the research
team can review interaction patterns and any points of confusion. Audio of your spoken
comments may also be recorded if you consent. You may ask for the recording to be paused
or stopped at any time.

### Is any patient or clinical data involved?

**No.** This study uses only **publicly available, de-identified EEG recordings** drawn
from open research datasets (the Temple University Hospital EEG corpora). **No patient
data, no hospital data, and no data from any clinical partner are used at any point.** You
will not see, handle, or be asked to interpret any identifiable patient information. The
task is a usability exercise on de-identified, public sample files only.

### Do I have to take part?

No. Participation is **completely voluntary**. You are under no obligation to take part,
and choosing not to take part will not affect your relationship with the research team,
the School, or the University in any way.

### Can I withdraw?

Yes. You may **withdraw at any time** without giving a reason and without any consequence.
You may also decline to answer any question or skip any task. If you withdraw during or
after the session, you may ask that the data collected from you be destroyed and not used,
**up until the point at which the data have been anonymised and aggregated for analysis**,
after which it may no longer be possible to identify and remove your specific
contribution. Simply let the research team know.

### Are there any risks or costs?

This study is considered **low or negligible risk**. The tasks involve only ordinary use
of a software application and answering questionnaire items. The main foreseeable
inconvenience is the time involved (about 30-45 minutes). If at any point you feel tired
or uncomfortable, you may take a break, pause, or stop. There are no physical procedures
and no clinical decisions of any kind.

### Are there any benefits?

You may find it interesting to see and comment on a research prototype. There is no direct
personal benefit and no payment for participation. Your feedback will help improve the
design of the interface and will contribute to the student researcher's thesis.

### How are my data handled, stored, and kept confidential?

- **Anonymisation:** You will be assigned a participant code (for example, P01). Your name
  will **not** appear in any recording filename, dataset, questionnaire response, or report.
  A single linkage list connecting your name to your participant code (kept only if needed
  to honour a later withdrawal request) will be stored separately and securely, and will be
  destroyed once data collection is complete.
- **Storage:** Recordings, task logs, and questionnaire responses will be stored on
  password-protected, University-approved storage accessible only to the research team.
- **Reporting:** Results will be reported only in **aggregated and de-identified** form in
  the student's thesis and any related academic outputs. No individual participant will be
  identifiable. Direct quotes, if used, will be attributed only to an anonymous participant
  code.
- **Retention and disposal:** Data will be retained in accordance with University of Sydney
  research data management requirements and then securely destroyed.

### Will I hear about the results?

If you would like a short summary of the overall findings, you may indicate this to the
research team and one will be provided once the study is complete.

### What if I have concerns or a complaint?

If you have questions about the study, please contact the student researcher
(`<STUDENT_EMAIL>`) or the supervisor (`<SUPERVISOR_EMAIL>`) in the first instance.

Any person with concerns or complaints about the conduct of a research study can contact
**The University of Sydney Human Research Ethics Office** using the contact details
applicable at the time of approval, quoting the project's ethics reference number once
issued: `<ETHICS_OFFICE_CONTACT / PROJECT NUMBER — to be added on approval>`.
